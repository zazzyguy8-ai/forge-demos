What your combination does

✕

Retinoid + AHA on the same night

Both increase cell turnover and both strip the barrier. Together they are the single most common cause of the red, stinging, peeling phase people mistake for 'purging'. Alternate nights instead — you lose nothing.

!

Vitamin C with an AHA

Both are low-pH and the stacking irritates most people. Vitamin C in the morning, acid at night is the simple fix.

✕

Too many actives at once (load 9 of a safe 6)

Each of these may be fine alone. Together they are asking a lot of one barrier. Cut back to two actives for four weeks, then add one back.

✕

No sunscreen with a photosensitising active

Retinol / retinoid and AHA (glycolic, lactic) make your skin more vulnerable to UV. Without daily SPF you are causing more of the damage you are trying to treat, and you will not see it for years. This is the most important thing on this page.

✓

Vitamin C + niacinamide is fine

You will read everywhere that these cancel out. That claim comes from 1960s research on unstable raw ingredients at high heat, not from finished modern products. Layer them.

!

Actives with nothing to repair the barrier

Everything you picked either exfoliates or stimulates. A plain moisturiser is not the optional step — it is what makes the actives tolerable enough to keep using.

Questions

Do I have to buy anything new?+

No, and that is the point. The routine is built from what you tapped. The only thing it will ever tell you to buy is sunscreen, if you are using something that makes your skin more sun-vulnerable.

Is this medical advice?+

No. It checks known interactions between ingredient types. It cannot see your skin or the strength of your products, and it says so. For persistent acne, rosacea or anything painful, see a dermatologist.

I heard vitamin C and niacinamide cancel out?+

They do not, in finished modern products. That claim traces back to research on unstable raw ingredients at high temperatures. The tool tells you this for free — it is a good example of what it is for.
